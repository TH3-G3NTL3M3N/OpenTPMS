OpenTPMS Rev 1 — Assembly Guide

First prototype build  ·  hot-plate reflow with low-temp paste  ·  written for a first-time PCB assembler

Rev 1 boards (PCBWay, 19×42mm, 0.8mm FR4 ENIG) · Guide version 2026-08-13 · Companion to docs/OpenTPMS_BOM.xlsx and docs/design-spec.md

The one-sentence plan: put tiny dots of low-temperature solder paste on every pad, place all the parts with tweezers, set the board on the hot plate until everything melts and self-aligns (~1 minute), cool, inspect under the microscope, fix anything ugly, then flash and test. You have 5 boards and 3 component sets — build ONE board first and don't touch the other components until board #1 works.

3 · Before any solder: board prep

  1. Test-fit your valve stem through the Ø6.0mm hole. Measured stems are often ~5.8mm and slide through as-is — if yours does (use the actual removable-seal valve you'll ride), skip to step 2. If it binds, enlarge toward Ø6.7mm (Outrider standard) with a round hand file, slowly and evenly; the nearest via is 4.2mm from hole center so 6.7mm is safe. Filing remains possible later, but it's easiest on a bare board.
  2. Deburr and clean. Light pass on the filed edge, then wipe the whole board with IPA and let it flash off.
  3. Inspect the virgin board under the microscope for 2 minutes. Learn what clean pads look like — you'll compare against this after reflow. Check the LGA footprint (U2) and module footprint for scratches or contamination.
  4. ESD sense: work on a non-carpet surface, touch something grounded before handling the module and ICs. You don't need a full ESD station for a hobby build, just don't shuffle across carpet in socks and grab the nRF module.

4 · Applying paste (no stencil — syringe method)

You're depositing paste by hand from the syringe. The universal first-timer error is too much paste. Target: a dot roughly half the pad's area, sitting on the pad, not bridging to its neighbour. Surface tension during reflow pulls solder onto the copper and self-corrects small errors; it cannot fix giant blobs.

  1. Warm paste to room temp (§1). Fit the finest tip on the syringe.
  2. Practice first: push 10 dots onto a paper towel until you can make small consistent dots on demand.
  3. 0402 passives: one small dot per pad. If you overshoot, drag the excess away with a toothpick.
  4. C8 (1206) and L1: slightly bigger dots, still under half the pad.
  5. U1 (MS5837): one modest dot per pad. Keep paste OFF the center of the footprint.
  6. U2 (LGA-12) — the critical one: the smallest dots you can make, one per pad. These pads are ~0.25mm; if your dots merge, wipe the whole footprint with IPA and redo. Under-pasting an LGA beats over-pasting it every time — thin joints work, bridges under the package are invisible.
  7. E1 (module): a small dot on each castellation pad on the board. The pads are half-exposed at the module edge, which means you can also fix any of them later with the iron — this is the most forgiving part on the board.
Take your time. Paste doesn't expire on the board in an hour. Do the LGA last so your technique is warmed up, inspect every footprint under the microscope before moving on.

5 · Placing components

  1. Order: passives first (C2–C7, R1, R2, C8, L1), then U1, then U2, then E1 last (it's big and easy to bump).
  2. Grip parts by their sides with tweezers, set them down onto the paste, then a gentle press so the paste grabs them. Don't squash — paste squirts sideways into bridges.
  3. Placement accuracy: within about half a pad is fine — molten solder pulls parts into alignment. Rotation matters more than offset for U2 (the LGA can self-align to the wrong 90° if placed badly). Verify the pin-1 dot under the microscope after placing.
  4. U1: confirm the gel port faces up and pin 1 matches silkscreen.
  5. E1: align the castellations with their pads by eye along both edges before pressing down.

6 · L1: the 0603-on-0402 special case

Rev 1's inductor footprint is 0402, but no 0402 10µH part can handle the radio's current — so you're mounting a physically larger 0603 (LQM18DN100M70L) on the smaller pads. The part will overhang the pads slightly on both ends.

  1. Paste both pads normally, place the 0603 centered so both end terminations contact their pads.
  2. After reflow, expect smaller-than-normal fillets. That's fine — verify both ends actually wetted (shiny connection between termination and pad, no gap).
  3. If it won't sit or won't wet: remove it and don't fight it. The board runs perfectly on the internal LDO with L1 absent — the firmware simply never enables the DC-DC (DCDCEN stays off). You lose ~30% TX efficiency, which is irrelevant for validating a prototype.

7 · Reflow on the hot plate

Sn42/Bi57/Ag1 melts at 138°C. You do not need an aggressive profile — low and slow is the whole point of this paste.

  1. Put the hot plate somewhere ventilated (flux smoke is unpleasant, not dangerous in these amounts — still, don't hover your face over it). Have tweezers and good light ready.
  2. Set the HP15 to ~100°C, place the populated board on the plate, give it ~90 seconds to preheat evenly. (This dries the paste and prevents solder balls.)
  3. Raise the setpoint to ~165–175°C. Watch the board, not the display — plate surface temperature and setpoint aren't identical.
  4. You'll see the gray paste suddenly turn shiny and liquid — that's reflow. Parts will visibly twitch and snap into alignment. Delightful, and normal.
  5. Once everything has gone shiny (the big module pads reflow last), hold 20–30 more seconds, then turn the plate off or lift the board off with tweezers at the bare board edges onto a heatproof surface.
  6. Do not touch anything for 2 minutes. Joints solidify around 138°C; nudging a part during solidification makes a cracked joint.
If a part is visibly wrong during reflow (tombstoned upright, drifted sideways, LGA sitting crooked): fix it NOW while everything is molten — a calm nudge with tweezers, the solder is liquid and forgiving. This is easier than any rework later.
Hot plate safety: it looks the same at 25°C and 175°C. Assume it's hot, keep the paste syringe and flux away from it, never leave it on unattended.

8 · Inspection (microscope time)

Check every joint at 20–50×. What you're looking for:

CheckGoodBad → fix (§9)
Passive filletsSolder wets both the pad and the part's end cap in a smooth curve. Bi alloy joints look slightly duller than regular solder — that's normal, not a cold jointSolder only on pad OR only on part; part standing up (tombstone); two pads joined by a solder band (bridge)
U2 LGA edgesA thin bright line of solder visible at each pad position along all four sides; part sits flat and levelPackage tilted; solder squeezed out at the edges (likely bridged underneath); a side with no visible solder line
U1 MS5837All 4 pads wetted, part flat, gel port clean and un-splatteredFlux residue over the port; part tilted
E1 castellationsEach half-moon pad shows solder flowing up the castellationAny castellation with a gap between module and pad
Solder ballsNoneTiny spheres scattered near joints — poke them off with tweezers/IPA swab; if many, your preheat was too short

Then two multimeter checks before any power:

  1. VCC to GND resistance (use the battery pad and a GND test point): must NOT be a short. Expect the reading to climb as the 220µF charges from the meter — climbing = good. Steady near 0Ω = there's a bridge somewhere; find it before going further.
  2. Continuity from each module castellation to its trace for the SWD pads at minimum (TP pads to module) — confirms the module edges actually connected.

9 · Rework — with a hot plate instead of hot air

Your rework tool is the same plate. Because the paste melts at 138°C, the whole board re-melts gently:

  • Wrong/failed part: board back on the plate at ~165°C → wait for shimmer → lift the part with tweezers → slide the board off → clean its pads with wick + tack flux (iron) → wipe with IPA → re-paste, re-place, reflow again. The other parts re-melt too but surface tension holds them in place — don't bump them.
  • Module castellation not wetted: no plate needed — dab of tack flux, touch the iron with a little wire solder to the castellation. This is normal iron soldering on an easy joint.
  • Passive bridge: tack flux on the bridge, drag the clean iron tip across it — the solder splits back to the pads. Wick if stubborn.
  • Suspected bridge UNDER the LGA: don't poke at it — remove the part (plate method), clean the footprint completely, re-paste with smaller dots, place a fresh LIS2DH12 (you have 5 spares at $0.92; the removed one goes in the maybe-later jar).
Budget for the MS5837: it tolerates about 3 reflow cycles and its gel port must stay clean and dry — no ultrasonic bath, no flooding with flux or IPA. Spot-clean around it only. If a board needs many rework cycles, pull the sensor off early and keep it safe.

10 · Cleaning

  1. The no-clean flux in paste and tack flux can technically stay, but residue makes inspection harder and coating adhesion worse. Spot-clean around joints with IPA on a swab.
  2. Keep IPA away from the MS5837's gel port — clean up to it, not over it.
  3. The battery pad must end up pristine bare gold — no flux film, no fingerprints. Wipe it last with a clean swab.
  4. Let everything dry fully before power.

11 · First power & flashing

Power the first board through the PPK2 in source-meter mode at 3.0V. It supplies power AND shows you the current in real time — the single best smoke-test tool you own.
  1. Wire PPK2 VOUT → board VCC (battery pad or VCC test pad), PPK2 GND → board GND. Enable output at 3.0V.
  2. A blank (unprogrammed) nRF52832 typically idles in the µA–low-mA range. Tens of mA or a climbing current = power off immediately and go find the short (§8 checks).
  3. Identify the four test pads from the Fusion 360 schematic before wiring: SWDIO, SWCLK, plus VCC/GND references (TP1/TP2 sit near the module, TP3/TP4 in Block B — confirm which is which from the schematic, don't guess).
  4. Connect the nRF52-DK's Debug Out (P20) header to the board with jumper wires: SWDIO→SWDIO, SWDCLK→SWCLK, GND→GND, and VTG (target voltage sense) → board VCC. Keep the wires short.
  5. Test the link: nrfjprog --recover then nrfjprog --ids / read the device — if the chip answers, your module soldering is proven good.
  6. Flash SoftDevice S332 + application when firmware is ready. For assembly sign-off, a successful --recover plus a blink or RTT "hello" is enough.

12 · Bring-up checklist (assembly sign-off)

  • Valve hole enlarged to Ø6.7mm, edge smooth, bore re-coated later with the strip
  • All parts placed per §2 table, orientations verified under microscope
  • Reflow done, every joint inspected, no bridges/tombstones/balls
  • No short VCC↔GND (climbing resistance)
  • PPK2 3.0V power-up: current sane, nothing warm
  • SWD responds (nrfjprog --recover works)
  • Test firmware sees both I2C devices: 0x76 (MS5837) and 0x19 (LIS2DH12)
  • Pressure reading changes when you gently press near (not on) the sensor / blow on it (temperature moves too)
  • Sleep current measured on PPK2 (target <5µA in deep sleep once firmware supports it)
  • L1 decision recorded: DC-DC works, or L1 removed → LDO mode noted for firmware
Only after every box is ticked: thin RTV coat on the exposed strip + valve hole bore + around (not over) the sensor port, then on to enclosure assembly (separate guide, once the CAD exists). And only then build boards #2 and #3.
